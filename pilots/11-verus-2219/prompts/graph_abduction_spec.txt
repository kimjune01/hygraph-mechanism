## The procedures you must execute (specifications — apply these, do not improvise)
You will solve this by **bi-abduction** then **tri-abduction** — the compositional spec-inference procedures from separation logic. Their specifications:

**Bi-abduction** (Calcagno, Distefano, O'Hearn, Yang; the basis of Infer). Given a required entailment that does NOT currently hold, `CURRENT |- REQUIRED`, infer TWO unknowns simultaneously:
    CURRENT * (?anti-frame) |- REQUIRED * (?frame)
- the **anti-frame** = the missing hypothesis that must be assumed on the left to make the entailment hold (the abduced precondition);
- the **frame** = the leftover on the right the entailment does not touch.
Solve for the **weakest** anti-frame that closes the entailment — the most general missing precondition, not one tailored to a single witness.

**Tri-abduction** (Outcome Separation Logic, Zilberstein et al., OOPSLA 2024, Sec 5.1). Bi-abduction composes sequentially; **tri-abduction composes two BRANCHES** that arise from a choice/effect. For branches with preconditions `P1`, `P2` reachable by different constructs, infer THREE unknowns — one shared anti-frame `M` that works for both branches, plus a per-branch leftover frame `F1`, `F2`:
    P1 * F1 |= M      and      M |= P2 * F2
The point: when the same branch can be reached more than one way, infer ONE shared precondition `M` governing the branch, not a separate condition per construct.
