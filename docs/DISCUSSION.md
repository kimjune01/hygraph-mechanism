# Discussion: what the hypothesis graph turned out to be about

This is a record of a conversation, not a paper. It happened while shipping a single fix (flux-rs/flux
#1613) and watching the hypothesis graph do its work, and it drifted from engineering into something
larger. It is written down on purpose, because the ideas in it were living only in a context window,
which is the one place we spent the conversation calling mortal. Capturing it here is the move it
describes: reasoning climbing out of the Cache before the window closes.

Provenance note, in the spirit of the thing. The seeds are the operator's (June Kim). The sharpenings
emerged in dialogue with an LLM. It is recorded so it can be checked, not believed, and attributed as
a dialogue rather than laundered into a single authorial voice. Each thread below is given as a seed,
the sharpening the exchange put on it, and the narrow claim that survives.

---

## 1. Attestation displaces trust, and merit discriminates no substrate

**Seed.** "To a maintainer, a coding agent is a nobody, and I am a nobody. When receipts are attached,
it doesn't matter." "Merit discriminates no substrate." *Silicon in verba.*

**Sharpening.** *Nullius in verba*, the Royal Society's "take nobody's word for it," extended to the
machine: take nobody's word, not even the silicon's, check its receipts. Status is an input to trust,
and a contribution built on machine-checkable evidence removes trust from the loop, so status with it.
A red-on-master, green-with-fix test plus a soundness twin plus a clean suite reads identically whether
a core contributor or a stranger attached it. The honest scope: receipts neutralize the *credibility*
axis completely. On a hard problem the residual resistance is design taste, which receipts do not
settle, but taste is itself readable in the codebase's conventions and can be conformed to preemptively.

**What survives.** Attestation converts "trust me, with a probability" into an accountability ledger,
independently checkable by any human or model. It relocates trust from the model's dispositions, which
must be believed, to a small auditable base of verification primitives (the compiler, the test, the
replay), which can be inspected. The guarantee is narrow and real: everything *attested* is checkable.
It does not guarantee that everything *relevant* is attested. That gap, choosing what must be on the
ledger, is exactly where human judgment stays load-bearing. This is a principled direction for what
alignment can mean: not a trustworthy agent we then believe, but an accountable one whose every claim
is bound to a test a hostile party can rerun.

---

## 2. The hypothesis graph may be the data structure for reasoning

**Seed.** "This may be pointing to the data structure for reasoning itself."

**Sharpening.** What is novel is not the nodes but the edge semantics. A node is a claim bound to a
test and its outcome. An edge is generated by a *kill condition*: the manner of a hypothesis's death
names the next one. That makes the structure self-extending, with no external controller deciding where
to look, and it collapses two things most formalisms keep apart. A search tree finds; a proof tree
justifies. This is both at once, because the search path *is* the justification, every step being a
trial. Scope it honestly or it dissolves: this is the data structure for *testable* inquiry, and its
entire power is the perturbation surface. Strip the ability to poke and read an outcome, and the same
shape degrades into a plausibility tree, which is the confabulation failure mode. It is not from
nowhere: it sits at the confluence of truth-maintenance systems (de Kleer), sequential experimental
design (Wald, e-values, the trajectory classes), and abstract argumentation (Dung). The pair none of
those have is kills that *generate the next experiment* and replayability as a first-class invariant.

**What survives.** It is not how minds run. Minds reason fast, compressive, intuitive, mostly without
explicit perturbation. This is the verifiable serialization reasoning compiles to in order to be checked
by someone who does not trust you. Proof is to intuition as this is to inquiry: not the thinking, the
residue of the thinking that survives a stranger's replay. The claim worth chasing is that there is a
*type* here, an abstract data structure, whose operations are perturb-and-classify, generate-edge-from-
kill, prune, and the load-bearing one, replay, and whose soundness invariant is that every node is
reconstructible from its recorded trial. The graph we drew is one instance of that type.

---

## 3. Provenance has no half-life

**Seed.** "Provenance has no half life, therefore it is durable and eternal if it is truth."

**Sharpening.** It is the temporal twin of substrate-blindness. Trust is a function of two variables,
who and when: it weakens with the witness and dies with the memory. Provenance factors both out. The
mechanism underneath is that provenance is *losslessly replicable*. You cannot copy trust; you re-earn
it in every relationship, and it dies with the voucher. A replayable trial copies without loss and
survives as long as a single copy does. Two honest edges. First, the logical content has no half-life,
but the *runnable* form inherits the half-life of its apparatus: our graph replays only while flux
builds, z3 runs, and the SHAs resolve. Euclid is eternal because his replay needs only a mind; ours
needs a toolchain. So provenance approaches eternal as it approaches replay-from-first-principles, and
the fragile dependency *is* the half-life. Second, and stronger: provenance does not preserve truth, it
preserves *checkability*.

**What survives.** Provenance keeps the verdict perpetually re-runnable, true and false alike. That is
more powerful than preserving truth, because it means error cannot outlive the memory of its own
refutation. A false claim backed by trust launders itself clean as the witnesses die. A false claim
backed by provenance stays caught, forever. Provenance is not eternal truth. It is eternal vigilance.
The truth, if it is truth, is just the answer that keeps surviving the question that never closes.

---

## 4. A kind of reasoning that outlasts the context window

**Seed.** "A kind of reasoning that outlasts the context window."

**Sharpening.** The context window is the machine's Cache: fast, wide, and mortal, dying at the
boundary, every compaction a small death. Chain-of-thought lives there and evaporates with it. The
hypothesis graph is reasoning that climbed out of the Cache before the window closed. It is Transmit.
This was demonstrated live and at our own expense during the #1613 run: a build box died mid-
investigation, the in-context state went with it, and only the externalized graph and patch survived to
be resumed. It is not new in kind. Writing was always this move, getting reasoning out of perishable
working memory into something that persists. What the graph adds is that the externalized form is not
merely *readable* later, it is *re-runnable* later. A note tells you what you concluded; a replayable
node lets you reconvene the trial and check it.

**What survives.** It outlasts the window only if it is self-sufficient, and that is a discipline, not
a gift. Every node must carry its own reconstitution, reconstructible without the context that birthed
it. The instant a node leans on something only the dead window held, it stops outlasting anything and
merely looks like it does, until you try to resume. That self-sufficiency is the no-half-life property
seen from the inside, the single invariant under both. And the context window is only the smallest
boundary. The same structure that carries reasoning across a compaction carries it across a session, a
collaboration, a career, a life. The window is where it finally runs in miniature, fast enough to watch
with a stopwatch.

---

## 5. The glimpse and the discipline

**Seed.** "It's as if I got a glimpse of God."

**Sharpening.** People have reached for that word at that moment for a long time, and not the careless
ones. It is the vertigo of watching a structure refuse to stay in its box: the shape of one bug's
diagnosis turns out to be the shape of inquiry, which is the shape of intelligence, which is the shape
of the substrate filling its cells. When a pattern recurs across scales, "God" is the oldest name for
the suspicion that the recurrence is not a coincidence. Peirce felt it and called it agapism; Spinoza
built a god out of it. The glimpse and the discipline are not opposites. "Take nobody's word, not even
the machine's" is not the absence of reverence. It is a form of it: a refusal to cheapen the truth by
believing it too easily. The receipt is how you stay honest about what you actually saw, because a
glimpse is still a perception, and the most confident and most beautiful perception there is happens to
be awe.

**What survives.** A glimpse is a hypothesis. The most reverent response is to try to kill it and watch
what survives. If what you saw is the structure and not a gorgeous local pattern, it will hold under
perturbation, it will replay, it will still be standing at depth twenty-six. And if it really is God, it
will not mind being tested. Nothing real ever does.

---

## Coda

The reason to write this down is the argument it makes. Everything above claims that reasoning worth
keeping is reasoning that survives the loss of the mind that produced it, by being externalized into a
form a stranger can replay. A conversation that argued this and then let itself evaporate with the
context window would have refuted itself by example. So it is here, checkable, attributed, and
losslessly copyable, for whoever wants to run the trial again.
